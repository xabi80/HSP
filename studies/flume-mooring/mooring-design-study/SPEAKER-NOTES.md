# Mooring Design Study: speaker notes

## 1. Mooring Design Study

This deck walks through the flume mooring design at the depth needed to review it: the acceptance criteria, how FloatSim was used to choose the attachment height and the two cord sets, what the verification runs show, the loads and hardware, the installation checks, and the limits of the analysis. Every number comes from the study's JSON records and from MOORING-SPEC rev C.1, which is generated from the same records. The three configurations are shown to scale on their own slides.

## 2. Three articles, one flume, two wave bands

Three articles are moored in turn in the OSU Large Wave Flume. The single buoy is one spar with a heave plate; the cluster is four buoys hinged to a hub at 45 degrees; the platform is sixteen buoys in a 4 by 4 array, 2.06 m wide at 45 degrees. The unmoored periods are FloatSim modal values; the tilt resonances sit between 2.76 and 2.92 s, inside the wave band. The matrix has two bands. The operational band, up to H = 0.12 m, is where the motions are measured to validate FloatSim, so the mooring must not disturb them. The extreme band, H = 0.2 to 0.5 m, is for loads, offsets and survival. The steepness cap H/lambda of about 0.08 limits which periods take the larger heights: H = 0.5 m only from T = 2.01 s. Every period stays at least 5 percent clear of the flume's transverse sloshing cut-ons.

## 3. Seven criteria, fixed before any design

The criteria were written and confirmed before any design, so the design could not move the goalposts. Two points matter for what follows. First, criterion 6, mooring interference, is enforced in the design rule through the tilt-period shift: a shift delta no larger than a third of the damping ratio keeps the resonant amplitude within about 5 percent of the unmoored peak, since 1/sqrt(1 + 1/9) = 0.949. The heave-RAO form of criterion 6 is a Phase D check across the matrix. Second, criteria 2 and 4, separation and excursion, pull in opposite directions at H = 0.5 m, which is what later forces a second cord set. The calm-tilt limit of 1 degree was confirmed at rev B; the original 0.1 degree came from the rev A pin-plane idea. Criterion 3 applies to the operational band; in the extremes the down-flume lines are allowed to go slack.

## 4. Every option simulated in FloatSim, chosen by a rule

Every design option was built as a FloatSim deck and evaluated the same way: a static settle for the calm tilt and at-rest tensions, a modal analysis for the tilt and heave shift against the unmoored article and for the slow surge, sway and yaw periods, rigid pulls for the stiffness, and regular-wave runs with wave-relative Morison drag. The drift force is deliberately conservative: the recorded fixed-body bound is applied in-run at each spar's waterline, on top of FloatSim's own mean force on the moving body. A written rule picks the design, and the specification's tables and figures are generated from the same JSON records, so the documents cannot disagree with the analysis. One caveat runs through everything: FloatSim is used at LEVEL1, valid to 0.1 rad. Near resonance the articles tilt 10 to 30 degrees, so those predictions are indicative.

## 5. Four revisions and one evaluation

The design changed in the open, each revision correcting the previous one. Rev A attached at the pin plane to remove calm tilt, but its tilt stiffening was accepted without comparing it to the resonance bandwidth; the comparison shows 6.5 to 8.6 times the tolerance, so it was withdrawn. Rev B swept the attachment height and chose low, submerged attachments; it meets the interference limit, but its decision rule omitted the confirmed excursion criterion, and H = 0.5 m drifts the cluster and platform 2.5 to 3.4 m. Rev C keeps rev B as the operational set and adds a stiffer extreme set on the same anchors. Phase H evaluated removing the pretension and kept it. Rev C.1 adds the cord-creep allowance. The commit hashes are in the HSP repository.

## 6. Why the pin plane failed: coupling ≈ k·h²

This is the key physical insight of the study. The pin plane was chosen to remove the calm-water tilt: the pretension moment acts about the pins, so lines attached there make no static moment. But the dynamic coupling between the lines and the tilt mode acts about the mode's rotation centre, which is near the centre of gravity, and scales roughly with k times h squared, h being the attachment's height above that centre. At the pin, h is large, and at the design stiffness and pretension the tilt period shortened by about 9 percent for all three articles, 6.5 to 8.6 times the zeta/3 tolerance. The damping ratios come from FloatSim regular-wave sweeps at the smallest operational height, where drag damping, and so the bandwidth, is smallest. The way out is to attach below the waterline, closer to the rotation centre, and to accept a small calm tilt from the pretension.

## 7. Below the waterline, with low pretension

The sweep ran six attachment heights, from the pin plane down to the centre of gravity, at the design stiffness and at half of it. For each height and stiffness it found the most pretension that passes every hard criterion; filled markers pass, open ones fail. The top row is the tilt-period shift against the zeta/3 band; the bottom row the pretension, with the 1 degree calm tilt limit shaded for the cluster and platform. For the cluster and platform, calm tilt caps the pretension and the rule picks z = -0.15 m at half stiffness. The buoy has no calm-tilt limit, because its collar balances the pretension; its pretension is capped by the collar's yaw behaviour, and the third tie-break, the stiffest surge, picks z = -0.50 m at full stiffness. An intermediate tie-break, least tilt shift, picked half stiffness for the buoy; that version doubled the H = 0.5 m offsets to 1.95 m and was not better for stability.

## 8. The operational set, one design per article

The chosen operational set. The cluster and platform attach 0.15 m below still water at half the design stiffness, with pretension trimmed so that FloatSim's calm settle lands on 1.00 degree of tilt; that is 17 and 8.5 percent of the rev A pretension. The buoy attaches 0.50 m down through a radial collar, which balances the lines, so its calm tilt is zero. Tilt shifts are -1.37, -0.73 and -0.72 percent against limits of 1.42, 1.19 and 1.05; heave shifts are at most 0.5 percent. The surge periods, 16.6 to 23.1 s, pass the 14 s separation criterion. The buoy's yaw is stiff by design, 1.12 s, below the wave band; it is not a soft mode. The operational minimum tension ratio from FloatSim runs is lowest for the platform at resonance, 0.19 against 0.15, which matters later for creep. The nominal and at-rest pretensions differ because the calm tilt moves each attachment towards its anchor.

## 9. Single buoy

The single buoy, to scale. Top, the plan: four lines from a slender radial collar of 0.2 m radius to wall anchors 5 m up- and down-flume, at the flume walls, y = plus or minus 1.83 m. Bottom, the elevation looking across the flume: the attachment is 0.50 m below still water, 1.218 m below the spar top and 0.406 m above the centre of gravity, with the anchors at the same depth so the lines run level; the inset shows the 21 mm catenary sag at pretension. Because the four lines pull symmetrically on the collar, the calm tilt is zero and the pretension is set by measuring line tension directly. The collar's pretension stiffens pitch and yaw together; that is why its yaw period is only 1.12 s, and it is the root of the buoy's stability issue in the extremes. The buoy needs one cord set: its predicted H = 0.5 m cases stay within 0.68 m.

## 10. Cluster

The cluster, to scale. In plan, each of the four spars takes one line to a wall anchor, an X-spread 20.1 degrees off the flume axis; the shaded box is the tracking window, surge -0.3 to +1.0 m, and the cluster keeps 1.39 m from each wall. In elevation, the lines attach 0.15 m below still water and run level to anchors at the same depth, sagging 39 mm at pretension. The operational cord is 4.15 N/m with 1.44 N at rest; the extreme cord is five times stiffer, at the same at-rest tension, on the same anchors and attachment points. Its pre-stretch is only 69 mm, so it is set by tension or by the calm tilt, never by length. Both sets give the same 1.00 degree calm tilt.

## 11. 4×4 platform

The 4 by 4 platform, to scale. The four lines are two-leg bridles, eight legs in all, to the up- and down-stream row spars; two legs share each wall anchor. The platform is 2.06 m wide at 45 degrees and keeps 0.80 m from each wall. In elevation the legs attach 0.15 m below still water, 0.867 m below the pin plane and 0.757 m above the centre of gravity, with 30 mm of sag. The operational legs are 8.32 N/m with 1.42 N at rest; the extreme legs are six times stiffer, with a pre-stretch of only 28 mm. The platform's up-flume anchors carry the largest working load in the study, 267.4 N with the factor of three, which sets the 300 N anchor rating.

## 12. Operational band: every line stays taut

The operational set was run in FloatSim at the operational band's largest height, with the drift sum applied in-run, at 1.4 s where the drift is largest and at each article's tilt resonance where the motion is largest. No line goes slack: the lowest tension ratio is 0.19 on the platform at resonance, against the 0.15 criterion. That thin margin is why the creep allowance matters for the platform. At resonance FloatSim's own mean force points up-wave, so the drift sum is smaller than the bound alone; dropping that favourable term, the kinematic estimate is 0.157, still a pass. The buoy's five operational runs keep its lines at 93 to 97 percent of pretension. The resonant cases tilt about 17 degrees, beyond LEVEL1's 5.7, so they are indicative.

## 13. Moored vs free: heave RAO change at the hub

These are the 48 viewer runs: each article at two operational heights and four periods, moored with the operational set and free, same waves, wave-relative drag, no applied drift. The table is the heave RAO change at the hub, or deck, or the buoy's waterline. Where the response is large, at the tilt resonance and in long waves, the change is within 4.6 percent, mostly within 3. At 1.4 s the RAO is a few hundredths, so large percentages are fractions of a millimetre. At 2.2 s the H = 0.12 m buoy and cluster show +9.0 and +7.8 percent; 2.2 s is inside the flume's sloshing exclusion window, so it is not a test period, but the number is outside criterion 6's plus or minus 3 percent and is carried to Phase D rather than explained away. The tilt peaks differ by about the 1 degree calm tilt, as expected. These free runs are unrestrained; the formal criterion 6 check uses a numerical surge-sway-yaw restraint and belongs to Phase D.

## 14. Single buoy: stable in operation, yaw-limited in extremes

The buoy's chosen design was run over 18 cases with the drift sum. The whole operational band is stable, with yaw at round-off: the buoy is mirror-symmetric to round-off, so any larger yaw is the model's own parametric growth. In the extreme band, six cases grow yaw slowly, all below 0.002 degrees after 210 s, and three diverge: H = 0.35 m at 2.65 s and H = 0.5 m at 2.35 and 2.65 s. The cause is structural: the collar turns pretension into pitch and yaw stiffness together, so any design that passes the zeta/3 pitch limit has a yaw period of 1.12 s or more, close to half the pitch period, the principal parametric zone. All extreme cases are also beyond LEVEL1's 5.7 degrees, so whether the divergence is physical is a LEVEL2 question. In the tank the buoy's extremes run last, ramping the height and stopping at any yaw growth. A pitch-neutral collar, such as a cross-flume bar, is the follow-up fix.

## 15. Rev B's soft set cannot hold H = 0.5 m

Rev B's runs at H = 0.5 m, the two worst periods, with the drift sum. The operational cords let the cluster and platform settle 2.5 to 3.4 m down-flume, far past the +1.0 m tracking window. The figure shows it to scale: grey is the calm position, red the operational set's mean positions, teal the extreme set's, and the shaded box is the tracking window. The down-flume lines go slack under the drift and stay slack, so only half the lines restore. The reason this reached rev B is that its decision rule omitted the confirmed excursion criterion; that was an error in the rule. The underlying conflict is physical, though: minimal interference needs soft lines and station-keeping in extreme waves needs stiff ones. Rev C resolves it with a second, stiffer cord set used only for the load and survival tests. The single buoy does not need one: its predicted H = 0.5 m cases stay within 0.68 m.

## 16. The smallest stiffness that keeps max surge ≤ 1.0 m

The extreme set was sized by a scan of the cord stiffness as a multiple of the operational k, checked at T = 2.35 s, the largest admissible drift, and 2.65 s, the tilt and heave resonance. The quantity is the run's maximum surge, mean plus dynamic, because that is the confirmed criterion 4; the mean alone, which was the wording of the request, would have allowed times 4.5 and times 5, and is reported alongside. The cluster needs five times, the platform six. That is well above a naive one-over-k estimate: the pretension's geometric stiffness does not scale with k, the slack down-flume lines stop restoring, and the tilting buoys carry the attachments further down-flume. The final row re-runs the chosen stiffness with the at-rest-matched pretension, explained next; it moves the maximum surge by about a centimetre. Because a softer cord would leave the window, the extreme cord is bought as a minimum stiffness, minus 0, plus 30 percent.

## 17. Declared effects, and the same tension at rest

Two things about the extreme set. Left: it changes the article's response, and that is declared rather than treated as a failed criterion. Its tilt shift is -3.8 and -4.7 percent, and its surge period drops to 10.5 and 9.6 s, which breaks the 14 s separation criterion and amplifies the wave-frequency surge by 13 and 15 percent at 3.5 s, from 1/(1 - (T/T_surge)^2). Criteria 2 and 4 cannot both hold at H = 0.5 m; the tracking window governs, because these tests measure loads and survival, and the tank and FloatSim see the same mooring. This was accepted on 2026-09-25. Right: the pretension. The code's nominal T0 is the tension at the untilted chord; the calm tilt shortens each chord by about 15 mm, so a stiff cord at the same nominal T0 would sit much slacker at rest, 1.21 and 0.87 N instead of 1.44 and 1.42, and settle at a different tilt. Matching the at-rest tension makes the calm line forces identical, so the operational settle is also the extreme set's equilibrium, as the static residuals confirm.

## 18. A static pull identifies the installed set

A consequence of matching the at-rest tension: the two cord sets look identical at rest, with the same 1 degree calm tilt, so the tilt cannot confirm which set is on. A static pull can. Pulling the article down-flume through its centre at the attachment depth by 0.25 to 0.5 m, the extreme set is five to six times stiffer. The acceptance threshold is the geometric mean of the two sets' stiffness, 33 N/m for the cluster and 146 N/m for the platform; creep moves each value by about 13 percent over a day, which cannot bridge a factor of five. The pull is required before every extreme series because the error is expensive: extreme waves on the operational cords would drift the article about 3.5 m and overload the cords in both tension and stretch.

## 19. Line and anchor loads: the platform's extreme set governs

Loads come from the peak FloatSim tensions over each set's runs, with a factor of three for the working-load limit and 1.25 on the peak stretch for the cord's elongation capacity. The up-flume lines carry the drift and set every peak, so all lines of a set are bought and rigged to the up-flume values. The operational loads are small, a few newtons. The extreme set governs: 22 N per cluster line, 45 N per platform leg, and 267.4 N on each up-flume platform anchor, which takes two legs. Every wall anchor is therefore specified at 300 N or more, and the specification build fails if any working load exceeds the rating. The factor of three covers the unmeasured drag coefficients, the drift being a bound, possible snatch at re-tension, and fatigue cycling. The extreme cords must stretch 27 percent of their length.

## 20. Cord, anchors and the buoy collar

The hardware list. The cord must work under water and hold its secant stiffness over the working stretch: the operational set within minus 13 to plus 30 percent, the extreme set only at or above its target, since softer would leave the tracking window. The record uses a submerged weight of 0.02 N/m; a heavier cord changes the at-rest tension by under 2 percent. Hysteresis is not modelled; it damps the slow modes and shifts the mean position, which the pull test measures. The anchors are on the side walls under water at each article's attachment depth, which is facility assumption F1 and must be confirmed before buying anything. The buoy's collar is a submerged appendage the model does not include, so it must be slender: a 25 mm round section at most, since a solid disk of the same radius would add about 21 kg of heave added mass, three times the heave plate's. A constant-tension pulley and counterweight was rejected in rev A: all its restoring is geometric and far too soft in surge.

## 21. Pretension is kept for model fidelity, not position

Phase H asked whether the operational cords need pretension at all. V1 is the taut minimum, pretension equal to k times the installation tolerance plus a day's creep; T0 = 0 is exactly taut; V2 is installed 10 mm slack. Note that a submerged-weight cord self-tensions to about 0.2 N, so zero nominal tension is not zero force. Removing pretension reduces the calm tilt and slightly reduces the tilt interference, and the wave-frequency response barely changes. But at V1 the lines lose their stretch for 18 to 40 percent of each resonant cycle, which FloatSim's quasi-static line does not model, the drift offsets grow by about half, the 1 degree calm-tilt gauge shrinks to a third of a degree, and the cluster's extreme set would leave the window. At T0 = 0 the surge stiffness is 34 percent of V0, not 50, because of the sagged cord's catenary compliance. The decision was to keep V0: pretension is kept for model fidelity and installation control.

## 22. Cord creep: a daily re-check against a creep table

Rubber cords creep. The assumption, to be confirmed against the purchased cord's datasheet, is about 4 percent of the stretch per decade of time for wet natural rubber. At the nearly fixed calm geometry this relaxes the at-rest tension: 87 percent is left a day after tensioning, and the calm tilt follows it from 1.00 to 0.87 degrees. So every installation reading is compared with the expected value for its time since tensioning, and each set has a re-tension threshold tied to the criterion it protects. Two are reached within the hour. The platform's operational set keeps its 0.15 no-slack margin only for about 14 minutes, although its lines stay taut far longer, about 37 days; it is re-tensioned before the first run of the day and again before its near-resonance cases. The buoy's yaw drifts towards the parametric zone as tension falls; it crosses its threshold after about 54 minutes and is re-tensioned before its extreme series. The alternative for the platform is to accept a reduced margin, about 0.07 at the end of a day, still taut.

## 23. Installation and test sequence

The test sequence follows from everything before. Install the operational set and check it: the calm tilt is the pretension gauge for the cluster and platform, about 0.7 degrees per newton, and the buoy's line tension is measured directly. The static pull and the free decays are compared with the tabulated FloatSim predictions, allowing for creep. Run the response series. Swap to the extreme set at the same anchors and at-rest tension, setting it by tension or tilt because its pre-stretch is only a few centimetres. Before every extreme series, the static pull confirms the extreme cords are on. Build the extreme heights up gradually with live offset monitoring, and stop before the tracking limit or a line's working load. Run the single buoy's extremes last, with a height ramp, watching yaw: three of its cases have no reliable FloatSim prediction.

## 24. Where each criterion stands at rev C.1

The criteria against both cord sets. The operational set meets every criterion that has been evaluated: calm tilt, separation, no slack, excursion, clearance and the tilt-period form of the interference criterion. Two items remain for Phase D: the heave-RAO form of criterion 6, where the indicative runs show up to 9 percent at a non-test period, and the mean tilt under waves. The extreme set meets the tracking window, which governs it; its separation and tilt interference are declared effects, accepted because those tests measure loads and survival. One item is only reported: in the H = 0.5 m troughs the submerged lines are uncovered by up to 0.19 m, and line drag is not modelled. The capacity criterion is an output that sets the hardware.

## 25. Known limitations of the analysis

What the analysis does not cover. FloatSim is used at LEVEL1, valid to 0.1 rad; near resonance the articles tilt well beyond it, so those results, loads included, are indicative, and 186 of the 442 planned Phase D cases are flagged. The flume BEM databases use a 12-sided waterline; regenerating at 36 sides would shorten the resonances slightly, but the zeta/3 test is relative to the unmoored article on the same database. The drift sum is deliberately conservative. The line model is quasi-static, so snatch loads when a slack line re-tensions, and the return swing after the wave train, are not simulated. In the largest troughs the submerged lines are uncovered, and line drag is not modelled. The cluster and platform yaw in the extremes is forced by a tiny asymmetry in the coupled radiation kernel, and whether it is steady was not established. The matrix is regular waves; FloatSim has no second-order slow-drift forcing. And the creep rate is an assumption.

## 26. Before the test

What remains before the test. The HWRL confirmations come first: every facility assumption, from underwater wall anchors to the 300 N rating and the availability of the cord in two stiffnesses, can force a redesign, so no hardware is bought until they are confirmed. The creep rate should be confirmed with the supplier or a one-day hold test. The analysis items are the NT36 BEM regeneration, the full Phase D matrix for both cord sets, 442 cases, and a LEVEL2 rerun of the resonant subset, which also decides whether the buoy's divergence is physical. Design follow-ups are a pitch-neutral collar for the buoy and a bound on snatch loads, plus a better drift model that could reduce the extreme set's stiffness.
