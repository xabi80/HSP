The single buoy, to scale. Top, the plan: four lines from a slender radial collar of 0.2 m radius to wall anchors 5 m up- and down-flume, at the flume walls, y = plus or minus 1.83 m. Bottom, the elevation looking across the flume: the attachment is 0.50 m below still water, 1.218 m below the spar top and 0.406 m above the centre of gravity, with the anchors at the same depth so the lines run level; the inset shows the 21 mm catenary sag at pretension. Because the four lines pull symmetrically on the collar, the calm tilt is zero and the pretension is set by measuring line tension directly. The collar's pretension stiffens pitch and yaw together; that is why its yaw period is only 1.12 s, and it is the root of the buoy's stability issue in the extremes. The buoy needs one cord set: its predicted H = 0.5 m cases stay within 0.68 m.
